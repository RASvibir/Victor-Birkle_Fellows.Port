Confidence vs. accuracy under production drift

Victor E. Birkle III · Started August 2026 · Related: OpenCity data research (2022–2024)

Question

In a production ASR → NLU intent-classification pipeline, under what measurable conditions does reported model confidence diverge from realized accuracy — and can those conditions be detected from operational signals alone (latency, audio quality proxies, utterance length, class prior shift) without privileged access to ground truth at inference time?

Narrow on purpose. Not a claim about frontier alignment. An empirical diagnostics question about a failure mode from production systems: high-confidence wrong answers under distribution shift.

Why this question

At OpenCity I cleaned multimodal training data, built regression checks for conversational models, and wrote log parsers for anomalous application state. The recurring pattern was not “the model is always wrong,” but confidence that stayed high while accuracy slipped when input conditions changed.

Research readiness here means: take that fuzzy production memory, turn it into a scoped experiment, measure something, and write down what failed.

Method (planned)

  • Fix a small intent taxonomy (8–12 classes) and a corpus with controlled corruptions (SNR drop, truncation, OOD phrases).
  • Train or freeze a simple, reproducible baseline classifier in Python.
  • Record per utterance: prediction, confidence, true label, and operational features.
  • Primary metric: reliability diagrams and ECE stratified by operational bins.
  • Secondary: which features best predict |confidence − correctness|; report what did not work.
  • Negative results welcome.
